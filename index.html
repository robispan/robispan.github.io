I build interfaces where the hard part is invisible. Most recently: reading a heartbeat from a phone camera.

68 bpm

A photoplethysmogram: the sharp rise is the heart contracting, the small dip after it is the aortic valve closing. HeartSnap finds this in the flicker of light through a fingertip. Illustrative — not a live reading.

Shipped · iOS

HeartSnap

Put a fingertip over the camera and flash. Every heartbeat pushes blood through the skin and changes how much light gets back — far too little to see, enough to measure. HeartSnap reads that flicker for fifteen seconds and turns it into a number.

The interesting part is what it refuses to do. A shaky hand or a half-covered lens produces something that looks like a pulse and isn't. HeartSnap would rather tell you it couldn't get a stable reading than hand you a confident wrong number.

Readings stay on your device and in your own private iCloud, which I cannot read. No account, no sign-up.
