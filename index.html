I build web interfaces, mostly Angular and TypeScript. On my own time I built HeartSnap — an iOS app that measures a heartbeat through a phone camera, and tells you when it couldn't.

A photoplethysmogram. The sharp rise is the heart contracting; the dip just after, the aortic valve closing. HeartSnap finds both in the flicker of light through a fingertip.

Shipped · iOS

HeartSnap

Put a fingertip over the camera and flash. Every heartbeat pushes blood through the skin and changes how much light gets back — far too little to see, enough to measure. HeartSnap reads that flicker for fifteen seconds and turns it into a number.

It also refuses. A shaky hand or a half-covered lens produces something that looks like a pulse and isn't. HeartSnap says it couldn't get a stable reading instead of handing you a confident wrong number.

Readings stay on your device and in your own private iCloud. No account, no sign-up.

It is a small product taken all the way, from signal processing to store metadata. Every decision is mine, and every shortcut shows up later.
