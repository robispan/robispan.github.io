How the measurement works

The flash shines into your fingertip and some of that light comes back out. Each heartbeat pushes blood in, which absorbs a little more of it, and the camera is sensitive enough to see the difference. HeartSnap watches that for fifteen seconds.

    One fingertip. Covers the camera and the flash — that’s the entire setup.

  2. 2

    Fifteen seconds. Hold still while HeartSnap reads your pulse.

  3. 3

    Your number, saved. Tag it, and it’s part of your history.

Why your camera can do this

Each heartbeat changes how much blood is in your fingertip, which changes how much light passes through it. Your iPhone’s camera is sensitive enough to see that change — a technique called photoplethysmography, or PPG. HeartSnap checks the signal is clean before it calculates anything: a wrong number would be worse than no number.

HeartSnap is intended for general wellness. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat any condition.

Your heart data is yours

There’s no HeartSnap account and no sign-up. Your readings are stored in your own iCloud account — HeartSnap can’t read them. Saving to Apple Health is optional and off until you turn it on. Nothing is linked to your name, account, or an advertising identifier. Full detail in the privacy policy.
